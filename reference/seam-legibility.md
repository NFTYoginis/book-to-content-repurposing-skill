# Seam-legibility

For material that isn't strictly from this book — a line the author said in a different context (a teaching episode, a talk, another book) — applied here to this book's thesis. Read directly from `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/social-reels/principle-bridge-reels.md`, a real three-reel production (pb-01/02/03) built entirely on this discipline.

## The risk this exists to prevent

Stated directly in the real trail: *"A viewer must never come away thinking Gabe spoke these words about [this book's subject]. He didn't — he said them [in a different context], and we're borrowing the principle."* An applied line, shown without a clear seam, reads as a claim the author made about this book's specific subject when he actually made it about something else entirely. That's a real misattribution risk, not a hypothetical one — it's the reason this discipline exists as a named, mandatory frame rather than an optional nicety.

## The two text layers — never blurred

Every piece of applied/borrowed content has exactly two kinds of on-screen or on-page text, and they must never be visually or verbally interchangeable:

- **The author's real words** — shown as a quote, attributed to its real original context (not the book's subject). This is what the audio/text actually is.
- **The brand's silent framing** — the chip, the bridge label, the disclaimer, the CTA. This text is never presented as something the author said. It's the brand's application, stated as an application.

## The mandatory frame — five elements, every time

From the real trail's own series frame, reused identically across all three reels so the seam reads the same way every time:

| Element | Real example | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Series chip** (persistent, small, top-corner) | `A TEACHING LINE — APPLIED` | States the seam up front, before anything else loads. Never absent, never buried. |
| **Attribution** (paired with the author's real quote) | `— from a yoga lesson. About stillness, not about fasting.` | Names the real original context explicitly, and explicitly disclaims the subject this content is now being applied to. |
| **Bridge label** (opens the application beat) | `Bring it to a reset →` | Signals the pivot from the author's real principle to the brand's application of it — the moment the seam is crossed, marked as a crossing. |
| **Standing disclaimer** (footer, persists from the bridge through the end) | `Self-directed practice. Education, not medical advice.` | Non-negotiable on every asset in the set once the bridge begins. |
| **End card** | Book card + CTA | Closes the seam the same way every time, so a viewer who's seen one piece in the set recognizes the pattern on the next. |

All five ship together. A partial frame (the chip without the attribution, or the bridge without the disclaimer) doesn't satisfy this discipline — see the refusal gate in `rules.md`.

## Applying this at any length

The real trail proves this at reel length (30–60 seconds, five elements, a full caption). The same discipline scales down: a 12-second caption still needs the chip-equivalent (a short parenthetical naming the real source), the attribution, and the disclaimer, even abbreviated. Length is not a reason to drop an element — see `rules.md`'s Never section.

## Worked reference — one full seam, start to finish (pb-01)

The author's real spoken teaching line: *"A yogi sits, and a yogi feels."* — said teaching meditation, about stillness, not about fasting.

The brand's applied frame around it: chip (`A TEACHING LINE — APPLIED`) → attribution (`— from a yoga lesson. About stillness, not about fasting.`) → bridge (`Bring it to a reset →`, followed by the brand's own application text: *"A reset is just sitting, in another form... Not flushing. Feeling."*) → standing disclaimer → end card.

Note what the bridge text itself does: it doesn't claim the author said anything about resets or fasting. It explicitly reframes — "a reset is just sitting, in another form" — making clear this is the brand's own extension of his principle, not a report of something he said. That's the seam staying legible even inside the brand's own copy, not just in the chip.
