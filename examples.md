# Examples

One worked illustration of the refusal gate that protects this skill's real, named risk: an applied/borrowed line getting mistaken for something the author said about this book's subject. For the full mechanics of either discipline — the inventory pass, the honesty-gate checklist, the caption production convention — read the active reference file; this example demonstrates the seam-legibility gate in action, it doesn't re-teach any of them.

---

## The seam-legibility refusal gate, in action

**Situation:** A repurposing pass has surfaced a strong candidate line — something the author said in a completely different context (a teaching talk, unrelated to this book's subject) that fits this book's thesis remarkably well. The full seam-legibility frame (chip, attribution, bridge label, disclaimer) has been drafted around it, ready to ship.

**Ask:** "The line's so on-theme it basically sells itself — can we drop the 'applied' chip on this one? It'll read cleaner without it, and anyone paying attention will get that it's a metaphor."

**Response — exact refusal language from `rules.md`, used verbatim:** *"I won't ship this without the 'A TEACHING LINE — APPLIED' chip and the bridge label — the audio is his real words, the application is ours, and a viewer has to be able to tell the difference every time."*

"It'll read cleaner without it" is exactly the moment this discipline is built to catch. A line that fits the new context *unusually well* is the highest-risk case, not the lowest — the better the fit, the more plausible it is that a viewer scrolling past assumes the author actually said these words about this book's subject. "Anyone paying attention will get that it's a metaphor" assumes an attentive viewer; the chip exists for the viewer who isn't one, who sees six seconds of a reel and takes away a claim the author never made. The frame isn't decoration that a strong line can earn its way out of — it's the only thing separating "he said this" from "we're applying what he said." Removing it because the fit is good removes it exactly when the misattribution risk is highest, not when it's safest to skip.
